# Framework Overview

## Purpose

This document provides a practical overview of the main frameworks referenced in this repository: ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 42001, NIST Cybersecurity Framework, and SOC 2.

It is intended to help readers understand how these frameworks support information security governance, AI governance, risk management, control assurance, and audit readiness.

---

## 1. ISO/IEC 27001

ISO/IEC 27001 is an international standard for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System.

### Primary Focus

- Information security governance
- Risk assessment and treatment
- Security objectives
- Control selection and implementation
- Performance monitoring
- Internal audit
- Management review
- Continual improvement

### Common Use Cases

- Building an Information Security Management System
- Preparing for certification
- Structuring security policies and procedures
- Demonstrating security governance to customers and partners
- Managing information security risks systematically

### Typical Outputs

- ISMS scope statement
- Information security policy
- Risk assessment methodology
- Risk register
- Risk treatment plan
- Statement of Applicability
- Internal audit programme
- Management review records
- Corrective action records

---

## 2. ISO/IEC 42001

ISO/IEC 42001 is an international management system standard for Artificial Intelligence Management Systems.

### Primary Focus

- AI governance
- AI risk management
- Responsible AI controls
- Accountability and oversight
- AI lifecycle management
- AI system impact assessment
- Monitoring and continual improvement

### Common Use Cases

- Creating an AI governance operating model
- Managing risks from AI systems
- Developing policies for responsible AI use
- Assessing AI systems before deployment
- Governing third-party AI tools
- Demonstrating responsible AI adoption

### Typical Outputs

- AI governance policy
- AI system inventory
- AI risk assessment
- AI impact assessment
- AI lifecycle controls
- Human oversight procedures
- AI supplier assessment
- AI incident handling process
- AI monitoring records

---

## 3. NIST Cybersecurity Framework

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework provides a structured approach for managing cybersecurity risk. It is commonly used to organise cyber risk activities across governance, identification, protection, detection, response, and recovery.

### Core Functions

| Function | Practical Meaning |
|---|---|
| Govern | Establish strategy, risk management direction, roles, policy, and oversight. |
| Identify | Understand assets, risks, dependencies, vulnerabilities, and business context. |
| Protect | Implement safeguards to reduce cybersecurity risk. |
| Detect | Identify cybersecurity events and anomalies. |
| Respond | Take action during and after cybersecurity incidents. |
| Recover | Restore capabilities and improve resilience after disruption. |

### Common Use Cases

- Cybersecurity maturity assessment
- Risk-based improvement planning
- Executive reporting
- Security roadmap development
- Cross-framework mapping
- Control prioritisation

### Typical Outputs

- Current-state profile
- Target-state profile
- Cybersecurity improvement roadmap
- Maturity assessment
- Risk-prioritised action plan
- Executive security dashboard

## 4. SOC 2

SOC 2 is an assurance reporting framework commonly used by service organisations to demonstrate controls relevant to customer trust and service delivery.

### Trust Services Categories

| Category | Practical Focus |
|---|---|
| Security | Protection against unauthorised access, unauthorised disclosure, and system misuse. |
| Availability | System availability for operation and use as committed or agreed. |
| Confidentiality | Protection of confidential information. |
| Processing Integrity | Complete, valid, accurate, timely, and authorised system processing. |
| Privacy | Collection, use, retention, disclosure, and disposal of personal information. |

### Common Use Cases

- Customer assurance
- Vendor due diligence
- SaaS security trust reporting
- Control readiness review
- Audit preparation
- Evidence management

### Typical Outputs

- SOC 2 scope definition
- Control matrix
- Evidence tracker
- Policy set
- Access review records
- Change management evidence
- Incident response evidence
- Vendor risk records
- Management assertions and audit support documentation

## 5. How the Frameworks Work Together

| Framework | Main Strength | Best Used For |
|---|---|---|
| ISO/IEC 27001 | Security management system and certification readiness | Building a structured ISMS |
| ISO/IEC 42001 | AI management system and responsible AI governance | Governing AI systems and AI risks |
| NIST CSF | Cybersecurity risk organisation and maturity planning | Structuring cyber risk improvement activity |
| SOC 2 | Independent assurance over service organisation controls | Demonstrating trust to customers and partners |

## 6. Example Cross-Framework Alignment

| Governance Need | ISO/IEC 27001 | ISO/IEC 42001 | NIST CSF | SOC 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Risk Management | Risk assessment and treatment | AI risk assessment and impact management | Govern and Identify | Risk assessment and control design |
| Policy Framework | Security policies and ISMS documentation | AI governance policies | Govern | Policy and procedure evidence |
| Access Control | Identity and access controls | Access to AI systems and datasets | Protect | Logical access controls |
| Incident Response | Information security incident management | AI incident and issue escalation | Respond and Recover | Incident response controls |
| Monitoring | Performance evaluation and audit | AI monitoring and post-deployment review | Detect | Monitoring and evidence review |
| Supplier Risk | Supplier relationship controls | AI supplier and third-party review | Govern / Identify | Vendor management controls |
