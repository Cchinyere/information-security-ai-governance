# AI Governance Policy Template

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to establish clear governance requirements for the responsible, secure, ethical, and compliant use of Artificial Intelligence systems within the organisation.

This policy supports the management of AI-related risks across the AI lifecycle and promotes accountability, transparency, human oversight, security, privacy, fairness, and compliance with applicable governance expectations.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All AI systems developed, procured, deployed, or used by the organisation.
- Employees, contractors, third-party suppliers, and business partners who use or manage AI systems on behalf of the organisation.
- AI systems used for internal operations, customer-facing services, decision support, automation, analytics, content generation, monitoring, or risk assessment.

This policy covers AI systems across the full lifecycle, including:

- Ideation and use case proposal
- Design and development
- Procurement or vendor selection
- Testing and validation
- Deployment
- Monitoring
- Change management
- Incident handling
- Retirement or decommissioning

---

## 3. Policy Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Ensure AI systems are used responsibly and lawfully.
- Identify, assess, and manage AI-related risks.
- Maintain an inventory of approved AI systems.
- Define clear ownership and accountability for AI use.
- Ensure appropriate human oversight of AI-enabled decisions.
- Protect confidential, personal, and sensitive data.
- Reduce the risk of bias, unfairness, security weakness, and misuse.
- Support transparency, explainability, and auditability.
- Ensure AI systems are monitored throughout their lifecycle.

---

## 4. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Artificial Intelligence System | A system that uses machine learning, statistical models, rules, generative models, or automated reasoning to perform tasks that would normally require human judgement or intelligence. |
| Generative AI | AI capable of generating text, images, code, audio, video, or other content in response to prompts. |
| AI System Owner | The person accountable for the business use, performance, risk, and governance of an AI system. |
| Human Oversight | Human review, supervision, or intervention applied to AI outputs, recommendations, or decisions. |
| AI Risk Assessment | A structured assessment used to identify risks associated with an AI system and define appropriate controls. |
| AI System Inventory | A register of AI systems used or proposed for use within the organisation. |

---

## 5. Governance Principles

AI systems must be governed in line with the following principles:

| Principle | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Accountability | Each AI system must have a named business owner and clearly assigned governance responsibilities. |
| Transparency | AI use must be documented, explainable where required, and communicated appropriately to relevant stakeholders. |
| Human Oversight | AI systems must include proportionate human review, especially where outputs affect individuals, customers, employees, or critical business decisions. |
| Fairness | AI systems must be assessed for bias, discrimination, and unfair outcomes. |
| Security | AI systems must be protected against unauthorised access, misuse, data leakage, adversarial manipulation, and model compromise. |
| Privacy | Personal and sensitive data must be handled in line with data protection and privacy requirements. |
| Reliability | AI systems must be tested, validated, monitored, and reviewed to ensure they perform as intended. |
| Compliance | AI systems must comply with applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, and organisational requirements. |

---

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Executive Management | Provide oversight, approve AI governance direction, and ensure adequate resources are available. |
| AI Governance Lead / Committee | Maintain the AI governance framework, review high-risk AI use cases, and monitor compliance with this policy. |
| AI System Owner | Own the AI use case, ensure risk assessments are completed, approve acceptable use, and maintain evidence of controls. |
| Information Security Team | Review AI-related security risks, access controls, logging, monitoring, and incident response requirements. |
| Data Protection / Privacy Team | Review personal data use, privacy risks, DPIA requirements, and data protection obligations. |
| Legal / Compliance Team | Review legal, regulatory, contractual, and ethical implications of AI use. |
| Technology / Engineering Team | Implement technical controls, testing, monitoring, and secure deployment practices. |
| Users | Use AI systems responsibly, follow approved procedures, and report misuse, errors, or concerns. |
| Third-Party Suppliers | Meet contractual, security, privacy, and governance requirements for AI systems or services provided. |

---

## 7. AI System Inventory

All AI systems must be recorded in the organisation’s AI system inventory before development, procurement, or deployment.

The inventory should include:

- AI system name
- Business owner
- Technical owner
- Business purpose
- Intended users
- Data sources
- Vendor or provider
- AI type
- Risk classification
- Approval status
- Monitoring requirements
- Review date

Unapproved AI systems must not be used for business-critical, customer-facing, confidential, regulated, or sensitive activities.

---

## 8. AI Use Case Approval

Before an AI system is developed, purchased, or deployed, the business owner must complete an AI use case assessment.

The assessment should consider:

- Business purpose and expected benefit
- Intended and prohibited use
- Data sensitivity
- Impact on individuals or groups
- Level of automation
- Human oversight requirements
- Security risks
- Privacy risks
- Bias and fairness risks
- Legal and regulatory considerations
- Third-party dependency
- Monitoring requirements

High-risk AI use cases must be reviewed by the AI Governance Lead or relevant governance forum before approval.

---

## 9. AI Risk Classification

AI systems must be classified based on their risk level.

| Risk Level | Description | Minimum Governance Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Limited business impact, low autonomy, minimal sensitive data, and no material impact on individuals. | Inventory entry and basic owner approval. |
| Medium | Moderate business impact, some sensitive data, or decision-support use requiring human review. | AI risk assessment, documented controls, and periodic review. |
| High | Significant operational, legal, ethical, security, or individual impact. | Formal governance review, enhanced controls, testing, monitoring, and senior approval. |
| Prohibited | Use creates unacceptable legal, ethical, safety, rights-based, or organisational risk. | Use must not proceed. |

Risk classification must be reviewed when the AI system changes, expands in scope, uses new data, or affects a new user group.

---

## 10. Data Governance and Privacy

AI systems must only use data that is appropriate, authorised, secure, and relevant to the approved purpose.

The organisation must ensure that:

- Personal data is processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently.
- Sensitive data is not used unless approved and adequately protected.
- Data used by AI systems is accurate, relevant, and minimised where possible.
- Data retention requirements are defined and followed.
- Confidential information is not entered into unauthorised AI tools.
- Privacy impact assessments are completed where required.
- Data sharing with AI vendors is reviewed and approved.

---

## 11. Security Requirements

AI systems must be protected using proportionate security controls.

Security controls should include:

- Access control and least privilege
- Multi-factor authentication where appropriate
- Secure configuration
- Logging and monitoring
- Data encryption where required
- Secure API and integration management
- Vulnerability management
- Change control
- Incident response procedures
- Protection against prompt injection, data leakage, model misuse, and unauthorised access

Security requirements must be reviewed before deployment and after significant changes.

---

## 12. Human Oversight

AI systems must include appropriate human oversight based on their risk level and intended use.

Human oversight may include:

- Review of AI-generated outputs before action is taken
- Approval of high-impact decisions
- Ability to override AI recommendations
- Clear escalation routes for errors or concerns
- Documented accountability for final decisions
- User training on AI limitations and appropriate use

AI systems must not be used as the sole basis for high-impact decisions unless formally approved and legally permitted.

---

## 13. Transparency and Explainability

The organisation must ensure that AI use is transparent and explainable where appropriate.

This may include:

- Informing users or stakeholders when AI is being used
- Explaining the purpose of the AI system
- Documenting how outputs are generated or used
- Maintaining records of key decisions and approvals
- Providing explanations for AI-supported decisions where required
- Ensuring AI outputs can be reviewed and challenged where appropriate

---

## 14. Bias, Fairness, and Ethical Review

AI systems must be assessed for potential bias, unfairness, discrimination, or harmful outcomes.

Reviews should consider:

- Data quality and representativeness
- Impact on individuals or groups
- Potential discriminatory outcomes
- Accessibility and inclusion
- False positives and false negatives
- Feedback and challenge mechanisms
- Ongoing monitoring for unintended consequences

High-risk AI systems must undergo enhanced fairness and impact review before deployment.

---

## 15. Third-Party AI Systems

Third-party AI systems must be reviewed before use.

Supplier review should consider:

- Vendor security posture
- Data handling and retention
- Model training practices where known
- Contractual protections
- Privacy and data processing requirements
- Service availability and resilience
- Audit rights or assurance evidence
- Incident notification obligations
- Exit or decommissioning arrangements

AI suppliers must not be used where their terms, controls, or data practices create unacceptable risk.

---

## 16. Acceptable Use of Generative AI

Users must only use approved generative AI tools for business purposes.

Users must not enter the following into unauthorised AI tools:

- Personal data
- Customer data
- Confidential business information
- Security credentials
- Source code or proprietary technical information
- Legal, financial, or regulated information
- Information subject to contractual confidentiality

AI-generated content must be reviewed before use, especially where it may affect customers, employees, compliance obligations, security decisions, or public communications.

---

## 17. Monitoring and Review

AI systems must be monitored throughout their lifecycle.

Monitoring should include:

- Performance and accuracy
- Security events
- User feedback
- Bias or fairness issues
- Data drift or model drift
- Unexpected or harmful outputs
- Compliance with approved use
- Incident trends
- Changes in legal, regulatory, or business requirements

AI systems must be reviewed at least annually, or more frequently for high-risk systems.

---

## 18. AI Incidents and Escalation

AI-related incidents must be reported and managed through the organisation’s incident management process.

AI incidents may include:

- Unauthorised AI use
- Data leakage
- Harmful or inappropriate outputs
- Biased or discriminatory outcomes
- Security compromise
- Incorrect high-impact recommendations
- Vendor-related AI failures
- Breach of this policy

High-risk incidents must be escalated to Information Security, Data Protection, Legal, Compliance, and senior management where appropriate.

---

## 19. Training and Awareness

Relevant employees and users must receive training appropriate to their role.

Training should cover:

- Responsible AI use
- Approved and prohibited AI activities
- Data protection and confidentiality
- Security risks of AI tools
- Human oversight expectations
- Bias and fairness considerations
- Incident reporting
- Limitations of AI-generated outputs

---

## 20. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy must be documented, risk-assessed, approved by the appropriate authority, and reviewed periodically.

Exception records should include:

- Description of the exception
- Business justification
- Risk assessment
- Compensating controls
- Approval authority
- Expiry or review date

---

## 21. Compliance and Enforcement

Failure to comply with this policy may result in:

- Withdrawal of AI system approval
- Removal of access to AI tools
- Escalation to management
- Disciplinary action where appropriate
- Contractual action for suppliers or third parties

Compliance with this policy may be assessed through audits, reviews, monitoring, and governance reporting.

---

## 22. Policy Review

This policy should be reviewed at least annually or when significant changes occur, including:

- New AI regulations or guidance
- Material changes to AI systems
- Security or privacy incidents
- Changes to organisational risk appetite
- New business use cases
- Audit findings
- Changes in technology or supplier arrangements
